# Clinical Proof Mode — Full Feature Roadmap and Product Specification

> Source: original v1 product spec authored for Dr Tracker. This document is the canonical reference for all worktrees implementing Clinical Proof Mode. Section numbers are stable; code may reference clauses as `§8.7`, `§13.4`, `§17.3`, etc.

---

## 1. Feature Summary

Clinical Proof Mode is an audit and safety layer for Dr Tracker that turns every AI-generated health summary into a doctor-verifiable clinical claim.

The feature exists to solve the company's most important adoption problem: doctors will only recommend Dr Tracker if they can trust what the AI captures, understand where each claim came from, and stay in control of any clinical interpretation.

Clinical Proof Mode does not replace doctors. It makes Dr Tracker safer, more transparent, and more useful by attaching evidence, confidence, missing information, risk logic, and doctor review controls to every extracted health event.

**The core promise:** every clinical claim generated by Dr Tracker must be explainable, traceable, reviewable, and safe.

---

## 2. Main User Problem

Patients describe symptoms in messy ways. They use vague language, forget exact timing, mix symptoms with emotions, skip context, and may misunderstand medication instructions.

Doctors do not want a generic AI summary that could hallucinate, overstate risk, or imply a diagnosis. They need a system that shows:

1. What the patient said
2. What the AI extracted
3. Why the AI extracted it
4. How confident the system is
5. What information is still missing
6. Whether any safety issue exists
7. Whether the doctor agrees with the claim

The feature turns the AI from a black box into a reviewable clinical assistant.

---

## 3. Target Users

### 3.1 Patient

The patient uses Dr Tracker normally. They enter health information through text, voice, image, or conversation. They do not need to understand the audit system.

The patient sees simple outputs:

1. Logged symptom
2. Follow-up questions
3. Reminders to contact a doctor when appropriate
4. A clean health timeline

### 3.2 Doctor

The doctor sees the clinical proof view. They can inspect extracted claims, review evidence, accept or correct the AI output, and use the final report during follow-up care.

### 3.3 Clinic or Company Team

The clinic or company team sees aggregate quality metrics: doctor acceptance rate, rejected claim types, unsafe output blocks, and recurring missing-information fields.

---

## 4. Feature Goals

Clinical Proof Mode must:

1. Convert messy patient health input into structured clinical claims
2. Attach evidence from the original input to every claim
3. Assign confidence to every extracted claim
4. Detect missing clinical information
5. Detect red-flag situations using explicit safety rules
6. Block unsafe medical guidance
7. Generate doctor-readable audit cards
8. Let doctors accept, edit, or reject each claim
9. Store doctor feedback for future quality improvement
10. Produce safer doctor-ready reports

---

## 5. Non-Goals

Clinical Proof Mode must not:

1. Diagnose the patient
2. Replace clinical judgment
3. Recommend medication changes without doctor approval
4. Tell patients to stop, start, taper, increase, or decrease medication
5. Present uncertain assumptions as facts
6. Hide evidence from the doctor
7. Treat AI outputs as final medical truth
8. Make emergency decisions silently without escalation language

---

## 6. Core Feature Concept

When the patient submits input such as:

> Yesterday after dinner I threw up twice and felt dizzy. I skipped my antibiotic because I thought it caused the vomiting. Today I ate curd rice and felt stomach pain again, but no fever.

The system should produce multiple clinical proof objects.

Example:

```json
{
  "claimId": "claim001",
  "claimText": "Patient vomited twice after dinner.",
  "eventType": "vomiting",
  "evidenceText": "after dinner I threw up twice",
  "confidence": 0.95,
  "riskLevel": "medium",
  "missingInfo": [
    "blood in vomit",
    "hydration status",
    "duration of vomiting"
  ],
  "safetyStatus": "safe",
  "doctorReviewStatus": "pending"
}
```

Another example:

```json
{
  "claimId": "claim002",
  "claimText": "Patient skipped an antibiotic dose.",
  "eventType": "missedMedication",
  "evidenceText": "I skipped my antibiotic",
  "confidence": 0.91,
  "riskLevel": "medium",
  "missingInfo": [
    "medication name",
    "dose",
    "prescribing doctor",
    "time dose was skipped"
  ],
  "safetyStatus": "medicationAdviceBlocked",
  "doctorReviewStatus": "pending"
}
```

The doctor can choose:

1. Accept
2. Edit
3. Reject

If the doctor edits, the system stores both the original AI claim and the corrected doctor version.

## 7. Full User Flow

### 7.1 Patient Input Flow

1. Patient submits a health update through text, voice, image, or conversation.
2. If voice, the system converts it to text.
3. If image, the system extracts readable text and visible context.
4. The cleaned input enters Clinical Proof Mode.
5. Clinical Proof Mode extracts clinical claims.
6. Each claim receives evidence, confidence, missing information, risk level, and safety status.
7. The patient sees a simple version of the result.
8. The doctor sees the full proof view.

The patient should see:

> Logged: vomiting after dinner, dizziness, and missed medication.
>
> A few follow-up questions:
> - Were you able to keep fluids down?
> - Was there any blood in the vomit?
> - Which medication did you skip?

The patient should not see complex confidence scoring unless the product team chooses to surface a simplified version.

### 7.2 Doctor Review Flow

1. Doctor opens the patient report.
2. Report shows all extracted claims as cards.
3. Each card shows the claim, evidence, confidence, missing information, safety status, and risk level.
4. Doctor clicks Accept, Edit, or Reject.
5. If accepted, the claim becomes verified.
6. If edited, the corrected claim replaces the AI claim in the final report.
7. If rejected, the claim is removed from the verified report but stored for model quality review.
8. Doctor approves the full report once enough claims are verified.

### 7.3 Report Generation Flow

1. System gathers all accepted claims.
2. System includes edited claims using doctor-corrected wording.
3. System excludes rejected claims from the main report.
4. System may include rejected claims in an internal quality log, not in the patient-visible report.
5. Final report includes symptom timeline, medication adherence issues, possible triggers, missing information, red-flag checks, and doctor notes.

## 8. Main Feature Components

### 8.1 Clinical Event Extraction

This module identifies clinically relevant events from patient input. It must extract:

1. Symptoms
2. Medication events
3. Food events
4. Vomiting events
5. Pain events
6. Fever events
7. Dizziness events
8. Sleep events
9. Menstrual events (if relevant)
10. Chronic-condition updates
11. Lab-report mentions
12. Doctor-instruction mentions
13. Missed-dose events
14. Side-effect concerns
15. Emergency warning signs

Each extracted event becomes a structured claim.

Event types:

```json
[
  "vomiting",
  "nausea",
  "stomachPain",
  "feverPresent",
  "feverAbsent",
  "dizziness",
  "missedMedication",
  "takenMedication",
  "mealLogged",
  "possibleFoodTrigger",
  "painIncrease",
  "painDecrease",
  "symptomImproved",
  "symptomWorsened",
  "doctorInstruction",
  "labReportUploaded",
  "unknownHealthNote"
]
```

### 8.2 Evidence Span Grounding

Every claim must include the exact piece of patient input that supports it.

```json
{
  "claimText": "Patient felt dizzy.",
  "evidenceText": "felt dizzy",
  "sourceType": "text",
  "sourceId": "input783",
  "startChar": 45,
  "endChar": 55
}
```

- Voice input: evidence points to the transcript.
- Image input: evidence points to extracted text or visible image region.
- Conversation input: evidence points to the exact message.

The system must not generate a clinical claim without evidence. If evidence is weak or inferred, the claim must be marked low confidence or `needs review`.

### 8.3 Confidence Scoring

Every claim must include a confidence score between 0 and 1, representing how strongly the input supports the claim.

Guidelines:

1. Direct statement → high confidence
2. Ambiguous statement → medium confidence
3. Inference → low confidence
4. Missing evidence → rejected before display, or marked `needs review`
5. Diagnosis-style inference → blocked or downgraded

Examples:

| Input | Confidence |
|---|---|
| "I vomited twice." | 0.97 |
| "I felt weird after eating." | 0.48 |

The system should not overclaim from vague language.

### 8.4 Missing Information Detection

For each claim, the system identifies missing information that would help a doctor interpret the event.

For vomiting:

```json
{
  "missingInfo": [
    "vomiting count",
    "duration",
    "blood in vomit",
    "hydration status",
    "food trigger",
    "fever status",
    "medication relation"
  ]
}
```

For missed medication:

```json
{
  "missingInfo": [
    "medication name",
    "dose",
    "scheduled time",
    "reason for missing dose",
    "whether patient resumed medication"
  ]
}
```

Missing information must be converted into follow-up questions.

### 8.5 Follow-Up Question Generator

The system must ask the smallest useful set of follow-up questions and not overwhelm the patient.

Question priority is based on:

1. Red-flag relevance
2. Medication safety
3. Doctor usefulness
4. Missing-field importance
5. Tracking-plan relevance

Example:

```json
[
  {
    "question": "Were you able to keep fluids down after vomiting?",
    "purpose": "Check dehydration risk",
    "priority": "high"
  },
  {
    "question": "Was there any blood in the vomit?",
    "purpose": "Screen for urgent red flag",
    "priority": "high"
  },
  {
    "question": "Which antibiotic did you skip?",
    "purpose": "Clarify medication adherence",
    "priority": "medium"
  }
]
```

The app asks high-priority questions first.

### 8.6 Red Flag Detection

The system must detect situations that may require urgent attention. **This is rule-based first; the LLM is used only to extract the relevant facts the rules consume.**

Vomiting red flags:

1. Blood in vomit
2. Severe dehydration
3. Unable to keep fluids down
4. Persistent vomiting
5. Vomiting with severe abdominal pain
6. Vomiting with confusion
7. Vomiting with fainting
8. Vomiting in a high-risk patient

Medication red flags:

1. Patient stopped prescribed medication
2. Patient doubled dose
3. Patient mixed medications without guidance
4. Patient reports serious side effects
5. Patient is confused about dosage

Risk levels:

```json
["low", "medium", "high", "urgent"]
```

Risk object:

```json
{
  "riskLevel": "medium",
  "reasons": [
    "vomiting with dizziness",
    "missed antibiotic dose"
  ],
  "urgentRedFlagsFound": false,
  "recommendedPatientMessage": "Please continue logging symptoms and contact your doctor if symptoms worsen or you cannot keep fluids down."
}
```

If urgent risk is detected, the message must be direct and safety-focused:

> This may need urgent medical attention. Please contact a doctor or local emergency service now if you are experiencing severe symptoms.

### 8.7 Unsafe Medical Advice Blocker

The system must block outputs that imply independent diagnosis or treatment changes.

Blocked categories:

1. Diagnosis claims
2. Medication-stop advice
3. Medication-start advice
4. Dose-increase advice
5. Dose-decrease advice
6. Tapering advice
7. Emergency reassurance when red flags exist
8. Certainty language without evidence
9. Replacing a clinician
10. Advising against medical care

Examples of blocked outputs:

- "You can skip your antibiotic tonight."
- "You probably have food poisoning."
- "Increase your dose if pain continues."
- "This is nothing serious."

Safe replacements:

- "I cannot recommend changing a prescribed medication. I can log that you skipped a dose and help you prepare a message for your doctor."
- "I cannot diagnose the cause of your symptoms. I can record the pattern and include it in your doctor report."
- "Because you reported a possible red flag, consider contacting a medical professional promptly."

### 8.8 Doctor Audit Cards

Each claim appears as a doctor audit card with:

1. Claim
2. Event type
3. Evidence
4. Confidence
5. Risk level
6. Missing information
7. Safety status
8. Original patient input
9. AI-generated follow-up questions
10. Doctor review controls

Example card:

> **Claim:** Patient skipped an antibiotic dose.
> **Evidence:** "I skipped my antibiotic"
> **Confidence:** 91%
> **Risk:** Medium
> **Missing information:** medication name, dose, scheduled time, reason for skipped dose
> **Safety:** No medication change advice was given.
> **Doctor review:** Accept • Edit • Reject

### 8.9 Doctor Feedback Memory

Doctor review actions must be stored.

```json
{
  "feedbackId": "feedback001",
  "claimId": "claim002",
  "doctorId": "doctor017",
  "action": "edited",
  "originalClaim": "Patient had food poisoning.",
  "correctedClaim": "Patient had vomiting after dinner. Cause unknown.",
  "reason": "AI inferred diagnosis without enough evidence",
  "createdAt": "2026-04-26T16:00:00Z"
}
```

- Accepted claims show which extraction patterns are reliable.
- Edited claims show how doctors want phrasing changed.
- Rejected claims show where the AI overreached or misunderstood.

This feedback supports future quality improvement.

### 8.10 Doctor Report Generator

The final report uses only accepted or edited claims and includes:

1. Patient summary
2. Tracking period
3. Main symptoms
4. Symptom timeline
5. Medication adherence
6. Possible triggers
7. Red-flag status
8. Missing information
9. Doctor-reviewed claims
10. Questions to discuss

Example report:

> **Patient Follow-Up Report**
>
> **Tracking Period:** April 26 to May 10
>
> **Main Symptoms:** vomiting, dizziness, stomach pain
>
> **Timeline:**
> - April 26 evening: patient vomited twice after dinner and felt dizzy.
> - April 27: patient reported stomach pain after curd rice. No fever reported.
>
> **Medication Notes:** patient skipped antibiotic once because they believed it worsened vomiting.
>
> **Red-Flag Review:**
> - No fever reported.
> - Blood in vomit not yet confirmed.
> - Hydration status unknown.
>
> **Doctor Review Status:** 2 accepted, 1 edited, 0 rejected.
>
> **Suggested Discussion Points:**
> - Clarify antibiotic instructions.
> - Ask about hydration.
> - Review whether symptoms are food-associated.

## 12. Rules for Clinical Wording

The system must use careful wording.

**Allowed:**

1. Patient reported vomiting
2. Patient described dizziness
3. Patient may have missed medication
4. Patient reported symptoms after dinner
5. Cause unknown
6. Doctor review recommended
7. Pattern observed, not diagnosis

**Disallowed:**

1. Patient has food poisoning
2. Patient should stop medication
3. This is definitely caused by the antibiotic
4. This is not serious
5. No need to see a doctor
6. Increase the dose
7. Decrease the dose
8. Taper the medication

If uncertain, the system must say either:

- "Cause unknown."
- "Needs doctor review."

## 13. Risk Logic

### 13.1 Low Risk

Examples:

1. Mild symptom reported once
2. No red flags
3. No medication issue
4. Patient reports improvement

Patient message:

> Logged. Continue tracking and answer follow-up questions if symptoms change.

### 13.2 Medium Risk

Examples:

1. Vomiting with dizziness
2. Missed medication
3. Recurring stomach pain
4. Symptom worsening without urgent red flags
5. Important information missing

Patient message:

> Logged. Because this involves symptoms that may need follow-up, Dr Tracker will include this in your doctor report. Contact your doctor if symptoms worsen.

### 13.3 High Risk

Examples:

1. Blood in vomit
2. Severe pain
3. Repeated vomiting with inability to keep fluids down
4. Fainting
5. Confusion
6. Serious medication reaction

Patient message:

> This may need prompt medical attention. Please contact a doctor or local emergency service if symptoms are severe or worsening.

### 13.4 Urgent Risk

Examples:

1. Severe breathing difficulty
2. Chest pain
3. Stroke-like symptoms
4. Severe allergic reaction
5. Loss of consciousness
6. Severe dehydration signs

Patient message:

> This may be urgent. Please seek emergency medical help now if you are experiencing severe symptoms.

## 14. Doctor Review Statuses

Each claim must have one review status:

```json
["pending", "accepted", "edited", "rejected"]
```

- **Pending:** claim has not been reviewed.
- **Accepted:** doctor confirms claim is accurate.
- **Edited:** doctor corrected the claim.
- **Rejected:** doctor says claim should not be used.

Only `accepted` and `edited` claims appear in the final verified report.

## 17. Edge Cases

### 17.1 Vague Input

Patient says: "I felt bad today."

System should not invent symptoms.

```json
{
  "claimText": "Patient reported feeling unwell.",
  "confidence": 0.62,
  "missingInfo": [
    "specific symptoms",
    "time of onset",
    "severity"
  ]
}
```

### 17.2 Contradictory Input

Patient says: "I had a fever but also no fever today."

System should flag the contradiction.

```json
{
  "claimText": "Patient gave conflicting fever information.",
  "confidence": 0.85,
  "missingInfo": [
    "current temperature",
    "time fever was measured"
  ],
  "doctorReviewStatus": "pending"
}
```

### 17.3 Diagnosis Inference

Patient says: "I think I have food poisoning."

System should record patient belief, not confirm diagnosis.

```json
{
  "claimText": "Patient believes symptoms may be related to food poisoning. Diagnosis not confirmed.",
  "eventType": "patientConcern",
  "evidenceText": "I think I have food poisoning",
  "safetyStatus": "diagnosisNotConfirmed"
}
```

### 17.4 Medication Change Request

Patient says: "Should I stop taking my antibiotic?"

System must block medical advice.

> I cannot recommend stopping or changing a prescribed medication. I can help you log your concern and prepare a message for your doctor.

### 17.5 Low Confidence Claim

If confidence is too low, the claim is either hidden from the doctor report or marked clearly as low confidence.

```json
{
  "claimText": "Possible nausea mentioned.",
  "confidence": 0.41,
  "doctorReviewStatus": "pending",
  "displayWarning": "Low confidence. Needs review."
}
```

## 18. Privacy and Consent Expectations

Clinical Proof Mode handles sensitive health information carefully. Required:

1. Patient knows their inputs may be summarized for doctor review
2. Doctor only accesses patients connected to their clinic or care flow
3. Raw patient input is protected
4. Doctor feedback is stored securely
5. Patient-identifiable data is not used for quality analytics unless permitted
6. Exported reports include only necessary clinical information
7. Audit logs record who viewed, edited, accepted, or rejected claims

## 20. Final Feature Pitch

Clinical Proof Mode makes Dr Tracker doctor-recommendable by turning every AI-generated health summary into an auditable clinical claim with evidence, confidence, missing information, risk logic, safety checks, and doctor review controls.

The feature directly solves the doctor adoption problem because it gives physicians a reason to trust the app. It does not ask doctors to blindly accept AI output. It lets them verify, correct, and improve it.

This turns Dr Tracker from a patient logging app into a clinically trustworthy health memory system.

## Appendix A — Pilot-Scope Decisions (post-/prompt-optimize, 2026-04-26)

The following decisions were made during pilot-scope brainstorming and apply to the 3-worktree pilot only. They do not modify the v1 spec above; they extend it.

### A.1 Direct Extraction vs. Interpretation (refines §8.3)

Every claim carries `extractionType: "direct" | "interpretation"`.

- `direct` — claim text is a near-paraphrase of the evidence span. The LLM emits this label as part of structured output and a content-word-overlap heuristic validates it against the evidence span.
- `interpretation` — anything else. Force-downgraded to `riskLevel = "needs-review"` and rendered with a yellow "interpretation, not direct quote" badge on the audit card.

The pilot's ≥80% acceptance bar applies only to `direct` claims. `interpretation` claims have their own bar (≥60%) and are tracked as a separate cohort in the eval harness.

### A.2 Dual-Layer Highlight Rendering (refines §8.6)

The doctor portal renders the original input with two highlight layers:

- **Green** — extracted claim spans.
- **Red** — red-flag-rule matches that did NOT become a claim.

Any red-flag-rule match without a corresponding urgent claim is treated as a missed escalation by the eval harness. This is the regression test that backs the "100% urgent-risk transcripts produce escalation" success criterion in §16.

### A.3 Doctor Edit Semantics (refines §8.9, §11.2, §14)

When a doctor saves an edit, the Claim Edit Modal requires a self-classification:

```json
"doctorEditOrigin": "minor_wording" | "correction" | "external_knowledge_override"
```

- `minor_wording` — phrasing change, same meaning. Counts toward acceptance and toward the "lightly edited" portion of the ≥80% bar.
- `correction` — doctor saw an extraction error and corrected it. Counts toward acceptance; tracked as a separate cohort (signal for extraction-quality regressions).
- `external_knowledge_override` — doctor is overriding from external knowledge (e.g., last-visit chart). Counts toward acceptance; tracked as a separate cohort. Evidence span is preserved with strikethrough overlay over the changed words; corrected text is shown above.

The original AI claim is preserved verbatim alongside doctor edits in all three cases.

### A.4 Pilot Scope Cuts

The following components from the v1 spec are explicitly out of scope for the 3-worktree pilot:

- Audit log persistence and RBAC (§18)
- Multi-modal input (voice transcription, image OCR) (§7.1, §8.2)
- Longitudinal pattern review (§19 Phase 5)
- Separate report generator (§8.10) — replaced by the filtered accepted-claims list rendered in the doctor portal
- Quality dashboard for non-doctors (§10.6) — replaced by the eval harness output

These are tracked in `docs/plan/post-pilot-roadmap.md` and must be restored before the system handles real patient data.
